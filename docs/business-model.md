# Business Model Hypotheses

## Core principle

Do not monetize the network in a way that slows network growth.

A strong starting hypothesis is:

> Connecting is free. Automation and enterprise capabilities cost money.

## Supplier side

Potential free capabilities:

- create trading relationships
- upload Excel/CSV price lists
- basic validation
- basic distribution

Potential paid capabilities:

- automated API publishing
- SFTP ingestion
- ERP connectors
- webhooks
- advanced mappings
- audit / SLA / SSO
- enterprise support

Supplier-side willingness to pay is expected to come from reduced administration and fewer bespoke customer integrations.

## Customer side

Potential free capabilities:

- connect to suppliers
- retrieve/export current prices
- basic API access
- basic history

Potential paid capabilities:

- automated ERP sync
- approval workflows
- change monitoring
- impact analysis
- advanced history and audit
- multi-organization capabilities
- enterprise SLA / SSO

## Pricing principle

Avoid pricing directly per trading relationship if that discourages suppliers from connecting all customers.

Better candidates to test:

- plan-based pricing
- automation level
- transaction/data volume
- enterprise capabilities

## Early adopters

Founding suppliers may receive temporarily free or deeply discounted paid capabilities in exchange for:

- active pilot participation
- structured feedback
- permission to use them as a reference
- introductions to trading partners
- help validating the canonical price model

Prefer time-limited incentives over "free forever" commitments.

## Biggest commercial risk

The buyer-side value is relatively intuitive if many suppliers are connected.

The critical hypothesis is whether suppliers experience enough value to participate before the network is large.
