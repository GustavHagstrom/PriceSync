# Validation Hypotheses

This document separates assumptions from validated facts.

## Problem hypotheses

- Buyers receive supplier-specific price lists in fragmented formats such as Excel, CSV, email portals and bespoke integrations.
- Buyers spend meaningful time importing, mapping and validating price updates.
- Price updates are sometimes delayed, causing stale prices in internal systems.
- Customer-specific pricing is common enough to make generic public price catalogs insufficient.
- Suppliers maintain multiple delivery mechanisms for different customers.

## Value hypotheses

- Buyers would strongly prefer one synchronization interface for many suppliers.
- Suppliers benefit from publishing once instead of maintaining many customer-specific integrations.
- Historical/versioned prices have operational and audit value.
- ERP and estimation vendors would prefer one PriceSync integration over many supplier integrations.

## Adoption hypotheses

- Suppliers will join if onboarding requires little or no custom development.
- Existing Excel/CSV exports are enough to bootstrap most suppliers.
- A small cluster of overlapping buyers can create enough pressure/incentive for suppliers to join.
- Free basic connectivity will increase network growth.
- Suppliers or customers will pay for automation once the network is useful.

## Market hypotheses

- Construction / house manufacturing is a viable initial vertical.
- Existing platforms such as Tradeplace demonstrate the network model but do not fully solve generic cross-industry B2B price synchronization.
- Existing EDI, procurement and PIM products solve parts of the problem rather than eliminating the N × M integration problem for customer-specific price updates.

## Critical unknowns

1. How many supplier price lists does a typical target buyer handle?
2. How frequently do prices change?
3. What percentage of price lists are customer-specific?
4. What source formats dominate?
5. How much manual work and cost is involved today?
6. What failure modes are most expensive?
7. How do suppliers currently generate/distribute their price lists?
8. What would prevent a supplier from joining PriceSync?
9. Which side has the strongest willingness to pay?
10. Which existing products already solve enough of this problem to reduce demand?

## Validation rule

Do not mark a hypothesis validated based only on positive reactions to the concept. Prefer evidence from current workflows, measurable cost, real data, pilots and actual willingness to integrate or pay.
